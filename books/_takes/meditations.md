I like *Meditations* because, despite being written almost 2,000 years ago, it makes you realize we’re still mostly the same and worry about the same things. Worrying about what others think of you, or about not working on something you enjoy, felt particularly familiar.

I found that comforting. You feel as though your problems are new, as if you’re only just discovering them. Then you read this and realize that even someone as powerful as Marcus Aurelius was worried about similar things. That’s refreshing.

It also changed how I process those worries. I like the idea of separating what’s outside your control, such as what people think of you, from what you can actually change. Act on what you can change, and let go of other people’s opinions. Easier said than done, but it helps to see it in writing.

The passage about not wanting to get out of bed has stayed with me. Sometimes you want to stay there, do nothing, and forget about the world. I took his response as a reminder that we have a duty to nature and to others, and that our work matters. Thinking of something I don’t want to do as my duty—what I’m supposed to be doing at that moment—does motivate me.
